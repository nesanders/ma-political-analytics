WAR vs. replacement level over time

Solid points are what {{ page.name }} actually got each year; the dashed line is what WAR expected given the district's lean and (when {{ page.name }} was the incumbent) the incumbency adjustment — plus local demographics or {{ page.name }}'s own campaign fundraising, for any year that data supports it. The gap between them is WAR. A vertical marker flags a year new redistricting maps took effect — incumbency resets to 0 there even for a real long-serving incumbent, since this site doesn't chase incumbency across a map change (see the methodology page). Primary races are plotted too, nudged slightly to the right of that year's general and drawn as a different shape (a diamond for a primary, a triangle for a special-election primary) since a primary's share is relative to its own field size, not a two-party baseline — see the shape legend. Click a point to open that race's district page.

What drives replacement level, by year

Same regression as above, broken into its own terms for every year {{ page.name }} ran: a baseline, district lean, statewide tide, and incumbency, stacked up to WAR's expected share — plus whatever's left over, WAR itself. For a year with a district demographics match and/or a matched OCPF campaign-finance total, the bar also gets an added Demographics and/or Fundraising slice (both can appear at once) — not the raw predictor value (which has no natural zero-effect baseline the way lean and tide's 0–1 shares do, and would make the bar disproportionately large purely from its units), but how that year's district or {{ page.name }}'s own fundraising compared to a typical matched race's, in real vote-share points. See the methodology page for the underlying coefficients. An uncontested year's bar stops at the model's expected share instead of the actual result — there's no WAR (residual) slice, since WAR itself isn't meaningful against a mechanically near-100% unopposed share. A marker again flags a redistricting year, where the Incumbency slice drops to nothing for that reason, not because the effect itself vanished. A primary's own bar sits beside that year's general bar, labeled directly above it ("General," "Primary," or "Special" for a special- election primary), and combines its equal-share baseline and incumbency (already interacted with tide and lean) into just those two slices plus fundraising and WAR — it has no separate lean, tide, or demographics term of its own. Click a bar to open that race's district page.

WAR (wins above replacement) is how much better (or worse) {{ page.name }} did than their expected share that year — what a generic candidate of their party would be expected to get, from a regression on district lean, statewide tide, and incumbency, plus {{ page.name }}'s own campaign fundraising for any year with a matched OCPF total (see the Factors column, and "Campaign finance" below). Expected share is defined whether or not the race was actually contested; WAR itself is null for an uncontested race and for minor-party candidates — an unopposed candidate's mechanically-inflated share isn't a meaningful gap from expectation. Adapted from Split Ticket's published methodology — see the methodology page for the full definition, citations, and the fitted coefficients.
