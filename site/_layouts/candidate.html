WAR v2 vs. replacement level over time

Solid points are what {{ page.name }} actually got each year; the dashed line is what WAR v2 expected given the district's lean and (when {{ page.name }} was the incumbent) the incumbency adjustment. The gap between them is WAR v2. Click a point to open that race's district page.

What drives replacement level, by year

Same regression as above, broken into its own terms for every year {{ page.name }} ran: the intercept, district lean, statewide tide, and incumbency, stacked up to WAR v2's expected share — plus whatever's left over, WAR v2 itself. For a year with a matched OCPF campaign-finance total, the bar instead uses the WAR v3 finance extension (same idea, with an added Fundraising slice for that year's logged total raised). That slice can look disproportionately large next to lean or tide's — unlike those two (naturally bounded 0–1), logged dollars typically run 7–14, so even a small fundraising coefficient multiplies out to a sizeable chunk of the bar. See the standardized comparison on the methodology page for a scale that's genuinely comparable across terms. An uncontested year's bar stops at the model's baseline expectation instead of the actual result — there's no WAR (residual) slice, since WAR itself isn't meaningful against a mechanically near-100% unopposed share. Click a bar to open that race's district page.
