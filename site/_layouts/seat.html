WAR (wins above replacement) is how much better (or worse) a candidate did than their expected share — what a generic candidate of their party would be expected to get here, from a regression on district lean, statewide tide, and incumbency, plus local demographics where this district's own Census match supports it (see the Factors column). Expected share is defined whether or not the race was actually contested; WAR itself is null for an uncontested race and for minor-party candidates — an unopposed candidate's mechanically-inflated share isn't a meaningful gap from expectation. Adapted from Split Ticket's published methodology, not an original metric — see the methodology page for the full definition, citations, and the fitted coefficients.

Primary results

Primaries are scored the same way as the general model above, but relative to an even split of that year's field rather than a two-party baseline: each candidate's share above or below 1 ÷ (number of candidates), decomposed into a baseline, incumbency (interacted with statewide tide and district lean), and campaign fundraising where a candidate's own OCPF match supports it. Special-election primaries are included and marked below; special-election generals are not yet part of this site's backfill. See the methodology page for the fitted coefficients.
