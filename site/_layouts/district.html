WAR is a regression, fit across every contested race in this site's backfill: actual share ~ a baseline + district lean + that year's statewide tide + incumbency (1st/2nd/3rd-or-later term). This breaks each candidate's actual share into exactly those pieces, plus whatever's left over — WAR itself, the part attributable to the candidate. {% assign factors = latest_contested.candidates | where_exp: "c", "c.war_factors" | first %} {% if factors.war_factors.size > 3 %} This district's own Census match adds one more factor here — {{ factors.war_factors | last }} — folded into a Demographics slice below, whose bachelor's-degree rate is estimated to swing with the statewide tide, too; that joint effect is split evenly between the Demographics and Statewide tide bars rather than credited whole to either. {% endif %} See the methodology page for the fitted coefficients, their uncertainty, and what they're based on.

WAR (wins above replacement) is how much better (or worse) a candidate did than their expected share — what a generic candidate of their party would be expected to get here, from a regression on district lean, statewide tide, and incumbency, plus local demographics where this district's own Census match supports it (see the Factors column). Expected share is defined whether or not the race was actually contested; WAR itself is null for an uncontested race and for minor-party candidates — an unopposed candidate's mechanically-inflated share isn't a meaningful gap from expectation. Adapted from Split Ticket's published methodology, not an original metric — see the methodology page for the full definition, citations, and the fitted coefficients.
